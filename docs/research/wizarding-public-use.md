# Wizarding-world elements a student club can use in public

Research for issue #113, feeding #119 (the wizarding theme inside VIC's charter). Researched 2026-10-22. This is not legal advice. It reports what the sources say. Anything marked **unconfirmed** could not be checked at a primary source.

## Short answer

- Warner Bros. publishes **no general fan or non-commercial licence** that we could find. The only published fan permission is narrow: the Wizarding World YouTube submission guidelines, which allow Warner and Rowling IP only inside content submitted to that channel.
- Those same guidelines say fans may not use the IP in "advertisements, contests, promotions, products, or organizations", or on sites primarily themed around the Wizarding World, without Warner's prior written permission. A recruitment form for a club is close to that wording. It is the strongest published signal, and it points toward caution.
- In 2018 Warner told US fan festivals that new guidelines bar any names, places or objects from the series. Organizers switched to generic magic themes. This shows how Warner enforces, even against free events.
- The lowest-risk route is to evoke the mood (night sky, parchment, owls, wax seals, candlelight, letters) with free-licensed assets and VIC's own words, and to avoid Warner-owned specifics (house names, crests, title lettering, character names, quotes, book text).
- VIC's back-office use of the four house names is internal and not public. The public form is a different case.

### Element by element

These rows apply the sources above. They are our reading, not a Warner statement.

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| House names (Gryffindor, Ravenclaw, Hufflepuff, Slytherin) | Names from the series. Falls under the 2018 "names, places or objects" position. No published permission for a public club page. A secondary source lists Gryffindor and Hogwarts as trademarked in 1999 (see section 2, **unconfirmed**). |
| House colours (red/gold, blue/bronze, yellow/black, green/silver) | We found no published statement about colours alone. **Unconfirmed** either way. Risk comes from pairing them with house names or crests. |
| Crests and the Hogwarts crest | Warner marks and logos. The YouTube guidelines list logos as IP needing permission outside that channel. Avoid. |
| Film title typeface and logo lettering | The title logo is a Warner mark. We did not find a primary source naming a font. Do not imitate it. (The widely shared "Harry P" font is fan-made. Its licence was not checked, so it is **unconfirmed** and not recommended.) |
| Quotes and book text | Reproducing book text is barred by the YouTube guidelines. The USPTO refused Warner's application to register a quote from the series, but that is a trademark ruling, not a licence to quote ([Gerben Law](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/?p=10043), secondary). Write original lines. |
| Character names, invented dialogue | Barred without written approval in the same guidelines. Avoid. |
| Film stills, posters, official art | Warner copyright. Avoid. |
| Generic ideas (owls, letters, wax seals, candles, stars, wands, parchment, sorting into groups) | No Warner source we found claims these. Use own artwork or free-licensed assets. We cannot call any specific design safe, so avoid close copies of the films' owl, letter or crest designs. |

## 2. Trademark registrations

- **HARRY POTTER** (word mark): one trademark database lists serial 97628610, registration 7133214, registered 2023-08-08, owner Warner Bros. Entertainment Inc., goods listed as baked goods. Source: [TrademarkElite](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/97628610/HARRY-POTTER). This is a secondary site. **Unconfirmed against the USPTO database**, which our tools could not reach. Other sources say Warner holds many more registrations, and the counts disagree (15+ for HARRY POTTER, 55 US marks overall). Treat counts as **unconfirmed**.
- **Gryffindor, Hogwarts**: a trademark-firm blog says both were trademarked in 1999 ([Secure Your Trademark](https://secureyourtrademark.com/blog/harry-potter-trademarks)). Single secondary source. **Unconfirmed.** Goods and classes unknown.
- To confirm: search the [USPTO trademark search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/). We did not find an Algerian registry result, so whether Warner marks are registered in Algeria is **unconfirmed**.
- Jurisdiction: VIC is in Algeria. We did not research Algerian copyright or trademark law, or whether Warner enforces there. **Unconfirmed.**

## 4. How others handle it

Found and read:

- **Imperial College Harry Potter Society** (UK student society) sorts members into the four houses at a Sorting Ceremony and runs chess, quizzes, a treasure hunt and a feast. The article does not mention Warner or licensing. [Imperial magazine, issue 53](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/be-inspired/magazine/issue-53/society/).
- **Chestnut Hill, Aurora and Ithaca festivals** (US, 2018) dropped series names and went generic after Warner letters. [Potterish](https://potterish.com/warner-bros-prohibits-harry-potter-festivals-in-the-u-s/?lang=en).

Searched but not confirmed:

- Lancaster, Oxford, Surrey and Keele societies show up in search results as using sorting and a House Cup. The pages we opened were redirects, a login page or an inactive notice (Keele says its society is no longer active). We cite none of them as confirmed.
- We found no student group that publishes a Warner licence or a fan-use disclaimer. Not seeing one in the pages we read is not evidence that none exists.

Takeaway: student societies that use house names for member engagement exist and are visible. We found no sign they hold permission, and 2018 shows Warner acting against even free events that used series names. Whether Warner acts against a small non-US student club is **unconfirmed**.

## 5. What this means for #119

- A published rule bars use of the IP in "organizations" and on sites "primarily themed" around it. Nothing we found grants a non-commercial exemption for a club recruitment page.
- Lowest-risk reading: carry the wizarding feeling through VIC's own palette, own illustrations and free-licensed assets, with no Warner names, crests, title lettering, quotes or stills on the public form.
- If VIC wants house names publicly, the only route found is written permission from Warner (the submission guidelines mention prior written permission). We found no contact for that. **Unconfirmed.**
- A disclaimer saying VIC is not affiliated with or endorsed by Warner Bros. or J.K. Rowling is sensible. It does not create permission.
